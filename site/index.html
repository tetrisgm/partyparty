The part everyone asks about

Two different delays. Only one matters on the floor.

People conflate them. We treat them completely differently.

Delay from the DJ · glass-to-glass

We spend this one.

🎧📱
~2–4s

How long after you play a track the room hears it. A couple of seconds is fine — nobody's A/B-ing against your booth.

Sync spread · dancer ↔ dancer

We keep this one low.

~200ms apart

How far apart two people standing side by side are — the dancefloor number. Every phone parks at the same point, so the room drops together. (Not phone-to-phone phase-lock; adjacent phones can't be sample-locked.)

For the skeptics

No app — and it still scales.

The usual reaction is "you'll need an app to broadcast to that many phones." You don't — the browser approach is the one that actually scales.

📄

File-serving, not 100 live streams

Your Mac encodes once into small chunks; every phone fetches the same files over plain HTTP — like 100 people loading the same image. A laptop does it in its sleep.

📶

The only real limit is your router

~160 kbps a listener → 100 people is ~16 Mbps. The ceiling is your Wi-Fi access point's client count — a good router does ~100. Same for any tool.

🚫

Multicast is the trap, not the fix

Browsers can't multicast, so "just multicast it" means an app on every phone. And Wi-Fi multicast is slow and flaky. Plain HTTP is more reliable and needs no install.

🔒

It survives a locked screen

WebRTC drops the moment a phone locks. HLS keeps playing in a pocket — so the easiest format to join is also the only one that fits a dancefloor.

In sync, lock-safe

The room drops together.

Every phone clock-syncs to your Mac, so the whole floor reacts on the beat instead of half a step behind. Everyone gets your direct signal, full-range, in their own headphones — no muddy room PA, no dead corners. And it keeps playing when the screen locks.
