The wedge

No speakers means no permit, no complaint, no shutdown.

The rules that kill outdoor parties almost all target one thing: amplified sound. Loudspeaker curfews, sound-device permits, dB limits at the property line, rangers with meters — they trigger on speakers blasting into the air. partyparty puts the music in everyone's earbuds instead, so there's nothing broadcasting outside and nothing to measure.

For the skeptics

No app — and it still scales.

The usual reaction from technical friends is "you'll need an app to broadcast to that many phones." You don't — and the browser approach is the one that actually scales.

📄
It's file-serving, not 100 live streams

Your Mac encodes the set once into small chunks; every phone just fetches the same files over plain HTTP — like 100 people loading the same image. That's how the whole internet streams. A laptop does it in its sleep.

📶
The only real limit is your router

~160 kbps a listener → 100 people is ~16 Mbps, nothing. The ceiling is how many phones your Wi-Fi access point can hold — a good router does ~100. That's a router question, not a software one, and it's the same for any tool.

🚫
Multicast is the trap, not the fix

Browsers have no multicast API, so "just multicast it" means an app on every phone — the exact thing that kills a popup. And Wi-Fi multicast is slow and flaky in the real world. Plain HTTP is more reliable and needs no install.

🔒
And it survives a locked screen

WebRTC drops the moment a phone locks. HLS keeps playing in a pocket — so the format that's easiest to join is also the only one that fits a dancefloor. No compromise.

📈
The next guest is free

Guest #200 sounds exactly like guest #2. No bigger PA, no extra transmitter, no extra headset — just one more phone on the QR.

💻
One Mac is the whole sound system

The Mac (or a cheap travel router) is the entire rig — versus rented silent-disco gear at ~$3,900 for a 500-person night, every night.

SonoBus and FarPlay are genuinely great — for musicians jamming remotely at low latency. That's exactly why they're wrong for a party. Hardware silent disco does reach a crowd, but it bills you per head, every night.
