Founder · builder

A note from me

I built this to help a friend throw a popup — then couldn't stop.

A DJ friend of mine kept getting shut down. Rooftop, beach, a park at golden hour — the second the speakers came out, so did the complaints, the permit questions, and once, the rangers. Renting a hundred silent-disco headsets each time was expensive and clunky, and every "just use this app" idea died the moment someone's phone locked in their pocket.

So I wrote something dead simple: your Mac becomes the party. It streams your audio over whatever local Wi-Fi is around — even a $30 travel router in the middle of a field — and guests just scan a code. No download, no account. It keeps playing when their phone is locked or they're texting friends. That's the whole trick, and it took a while to get right.

I'm sharing it free because it's genuinely useful and I want honest feedback. Below I've split out exactly what works today from what's still coming — no roadmap theater. If a night goes sideways, I want to hear about it.

How a night works

Four steps, about a minute of setup.

Open the menu bar

Launch partyparty on your Mac. It sits up in the menu bar, pick your audio source and you're staged.

Hit Go Live

It captures your Mac's output and starts broadcasting over the local network. A QR code pops up.

Guests scan

They join the Wi-Fi and scan. The stream opens in their browser — no app, no account, phone stays in pocket.

The room drops together

Every phone parks the same distance behind live, so the crowd moves as one. You just DJ.

The honest engineering bit

There are two "latencies." Only one of them matters on the dancefloor.

People conflate these constantly, so here's the straight version. We deliberately spend one and keep the other small.

🔵 Latency A · we spend this

DJ → listener (“glass-to-glass”)

From the moment you play a track to the moment a guest hears it.

2–4s

This one we happily trade away. A couple seconds of buffer is what makes playback smooth and lets a locked phone keep going without stutter. Nobody on the floor can tell the set is a few seconds behind the deck — there's no PA to compare it to.

🟢 Latency B · we keep this low

Listener ↔ listener (“sync spread”)

How far apart two people standing side by side are hearing the same beat.

~200ms aspirational

This is the number that decides whether a room feels together. Every phone parks at the same server hold-back, so they all sit the exact same distance behind live — which means they sit right next to each other.

Straight talk on sync: you can't actively phase-lock a locked iPhone — the browser won't let you nudge its clock in the background. So a stable, shared buffer is the sync. The goal is honest and specific: the room drops together. That's not phone-to-phone phase-lock down to the sample, and we won't pretend it is.

For the skeptics

“No app for guests” and “it scales” aren't a contradiction.

Here's the usual objection: to sync a big crowd you need a real-time protocol, and to run real-time protocols in a browser you basically need a native app on every phone. That's the trap. So I didn't do it that way.

partyparty is just static-file HTTP serving. The Mac encodes the audio once, into small chunks, and every phone fetches the same chunks over plain HTTP — exactly like loading images on a webpage. There's no per-listener stream to spin up, so adding the 50th guest costs about what the 5th did. The real ceiling is how many devices your Wi-Fi access point can hold — roughly 100 on a good router.

Why not multicast or WebRTC? Both are traps here. Browsers can't multicast — you'd need a native app. And WebRTC dies the moment a phone locks. A shared static buffer is what survives a pocket. Boring on purpose.
