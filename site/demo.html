How it works

Three taps to a silent-disco popup.

You bring a Mac and a Wi-Fi network — your Mac's hotspot, or a ~$30 travel router. That's the entire rig. No booth, no cables to the crowd, no cloud.

STEP 01

Hit Go Live

From the menu bar, partyparty grabs whatever's playing on your Mac and starts broadcasting it over the local network.

STEP 02

Guests scan the QR

They join your Wi-Fi and scan one code. It opens a plain web page — iPhone or Android, Safari or Chrome. No download, no account.

STEP 03

Earbuds in — dance

Your direct signal plays in everyone's own earbuds. It keeps playing when a phone is locked, in another tab, or mid-text with a friend.

The dancefloor physics

There are two latencies. Only one matters on the floor.

Most streaming apps obsess over the wrong one. Here's both — watch the pulse leave the deck, then watch where the phones land.

LATENCY A · DJ → LISTENER

Glass-to-glass

2–4s

Time from the DJ playing to you hearing it. We spend this on purpose — a couple of seconds buys a rock-solid buffer. Nobody on a dancefloor can feel a 3-second offset from the booth.

LATENCY B · LISTENER ↔ LISTENER

Sync spread ← the one that matters

~200ms

How far apart two people dancing side by side are. This is the dancefloor metric, kept low by design: every phone parks at the same server hold-back, so all sit the same distance behind live. The room drops together.

You can't actively phase-lock a locked iPhone — so a stable buffer is the sync. Honest target: sub-second, ~200ms aspirational. Not phone-to-phone phase-lock, and we'll never claim it is.

For the skeptic

No app — and it still scales.

If you know audio streaming, your first instinct is "browsers can't do this at crowd scale." Here's exactly why the boring approach wins and the clever ones are traps.

✓ Why static HTTP wins

partyparty encodes the stream once and serves it as small static files over plain HTTP. Every phone requests the same chunks — the exact thing web servers and phone browsers have been ruthlessly optimized to do for 25 years.

✓No per-listener CPU. Encode work is flat whether it's 5 phones or 90.
✓Locked-phone safe. Standard media playback keeps running with the screen off.
✓The real ceiling is the Wi-Fi. A good access point handles ~100 clients — that's your crowd limit, not the software's.

✕ Why the "clever" ways are traps

The approaches that sound more real-time all break the two things that matter: no app, and it survives a locked screen.

✕Multicast. Browsers can't receive it. The moment you need it, you need a native app on every phone — and the whole "no app" promise is dead.
✕WebRTC. Great for a 1:1 call, but it dies when the phone locks or backgrounds — exactly when your guest pockets it to dance.
✕Chasing phase-lock. You can't command a sleeping iPhone. A stable shared buffer is the honest, robust way to make the room drop together.
