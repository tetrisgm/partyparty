01The whole idea

The set that never gets shut down.

Speakers in the air are what end the night — the permit you don't have, the neighbor who calls, the ranger with the flashlight. So take the speakers out of the air. Your set plays straight into everyone's own earbuds over a little Wi-Fi network you brought with you. No amplified sound. Nothing to complain about. Nothing to shut down.

No rented headsets. No app to install. No internet. Your guests point a camera at a QR code and they're in — iPhone, Android, whatever's in their pocket. It just plays.

Fully offline
No bars. No signal. No problem.

Runs on a bare local network — your Mac, or a $30 travel router in your bag. Dead zone in the desert, basement with no service, a boat: if you can turn on Wi-Fi, you can throw the party. The internet was never invited.

No app for guests
A hundred people. Zero downloads.

The moment a party needs an install, half the room bails. So there's no install. They join the Wi-Fi, scan a code, and they're dancing before the download bar would've finished.

03The part engineers ask about

Two clocks. Only one matters on the floor.

Everyone worries about "latency" like it's a single number. It isn't. There are two, they do different jobs, and knowing which is which is the whole trick.

A

Glass-to-glass

DJ → your ears · we spend this

The gap between the DJ playing a beat and you hearing it. We deliberately spend 2–4 seconds here — that buffer is what makes the stream rock-solid on a phone in your pocket. Nobody standing on a dancefloor can feel a two-second head start on the DJ. So we don't chase it.

DJ plays You hear it
2–4s of head-start we happily give up for stability.
B

Sync spread

You ↔ the dancer beside you · kept low

How far apart two people dancing shoulder-to-shoulder are. This is the dancefloor metric — and it's the one we keep tight. Every phone parks at the same server hold-back, so everyone sits the exact same distance behind live. Same distance behind = same moment as each other. The room drops together.

You Them
The room drops together — sub-second, ~200ms the aim.
Straight talk: a stable buffer is the sync. You can't actively phase-lock a locked iPhone — the browser won't let you. So instead of pretending to, we hold everyone the same amount behind live and let physics do the rest. The goal is "the room drops together," not phone-to-phone perfection. We say what we mean.
04For the skeptic

No app — and it still scales.

The magic isn't magic. It's boring, and boring is why it holds. partyparty encodes your set once and serves it as plain static files over HTTP. Every phone fetches the same little chunks — the same way every phone loads the same webpage. One listener or a full rooftop, the Mac barely notices.

1×Encode once, everyone fetches the same chunks. The DJ's Mac isn't rendering a private stream per guest — it's a file server.
~100The real ceiling is the Wi-Fi, not us. On a good access point that's roughly a hundred phones — the router's client count, not our software, is the wall.
$0No per-head cost. No headset rental, no cloud bill that grows with the guest list. The crowd is free.
Why not the "clever" way?

Multicast and WebRTC are traps. Multicast looks efficient on paper — but browsers can't do it, so you'd be forcing an app install on every guest, and the whole point was no app.

WebRTC gives you low latency until the moment it matters most: the screen locks, the phone throttles the connection, and the audio dies mid-song. A dumb, static-file stream survives a locked phone. A clever one doesn't. We chose the one that keeps playing while your guests are texting.

The honest fine print. partyparty defeats the need for an amplified-sound permit and the noise complaints that come from speaker volume — because there are no speakers in the air. It does not get you around residential curfews, the noise a loud crowd makes on its own, or music-licensing obligations, and it is not "legal anywhere." Audio is your direct signal in each guest's own earbuds — better and more consistent than a muddy room PA with dead spots, delivered straight to their ears. Sync means the room drops together — everyone the same distance behind live — not same-instant, phase-locked, per-phone perfection. The Event Hub (auto-saved replay, event page, moderated photo/video/comment wall, follow-the-DJ, linked socials) is coming soon and not part of today's release.
