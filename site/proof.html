For the DJ who's read the docs

“Isn't this just SonoBus?”

No — and the difference is architectural, not marketing. SonoBus and FarPlay are musician jam tools. They open a low-latency two-way audio session between people who each install an app. That's the wrong shape for a dancefloor.

SonoBus's own docs say a 25-member choir “is unlikely to succeed.”2 FarPlay's free tier is 2 people, 45 minutes, desktop only.3 Neither runs in a phone browser. Neither survives a locked screen.

partyparty is one-to-many static-file HTTP: encode the audio once, every phone fetches the same chunks in the browser. That's why it scales to a room, why there's no app, and why it keeps playing when a guest locks their phone to text a friend. Keep scrolling — every claim below carries a number.

The metric that actually matters

Two latencies. Don't confuse them.

Every streaming pitch mumbles about “low latency.” There are actually two clocks, and only one is the dancefloor metric. Here's exactly what we optimize and what we don't.

● Latency A · glass-to-glass

DJ → listener

Time from the DJ playing a beat to you hearing it

This is the delay we deliberately spend. Capturing, encoding, chunking and buffering the stream costs a couple of seconds — and it does not matter, because nobody's comparing what they hear to the DJ's monitor.

✓ We spend this on purpose. 2–4s buys a rock-solid, gap-free stream.
● Latency B · sync spread

Listener ↔ listener

How far apart two dancers standing side by side are

This is the number the dancefloor feels. Every phone parks at the same server hold-back, so they all sit the same distance behind live — which keeps two dancers close together. A stable, shared buffer is the sync.

◎ Kept low by design. Honest target: the room drops together.
Straight talk on sync

You can't actively phase-lock a locked iPhone from a web page — nobody can. So we don't claim phone-to-phone “perfect sync.” We aim for a sub-second, ~200ms aspirational spread so the room drops together. That's the honest, achievable version of a synced dancefloor — and it's the one that survives real phones.

For the skeptic · the architecture

“No app” and it still scales. Here's why.

The counterintuitive part: the constraint that makes partyparty easy for guests is the same one that makes it scale. And the “obvious” alternatives are the traps.

✓

Encode once, fetch many

The DJ's audio is chunked into small static files. Every phone requests the same chunks — the exact model that lets one web server feed a huge audience.

✓

The ceiling is Wi-Fi, not software

Serving files is cheap. The real limit is how many clients your access point holds — ~100 on a good router. Add a better AP, get more headroom.

✕

Trap: multicast

Elegant on paper. But browsers can't multicast — you'd have to ship a native app to every guest. That's the friction we refuse.

✕

Trap: WebRTC

Great for calls, wrong for this: a WebRTC stream dies when the phone locks. It's peer-session plumbing, not a broadcast that survives a pocket.
